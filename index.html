Perspective Geometry in Real Photographs

Sampurna • UG 2023 ug_2023@ashoka.edu.in Professor Ragvendra Singh Spring 2026 📷 Sony DSC S750 & iPhone 13
This project investigates how camera position and projection geometry influence visual appearance. Using an iPhone 13, I photographed the same scenes from different distances while adjusting zoom to maintain similar framing. The results illustrate three classical effects from computer vision: perspective distortion in portraits, telephoto compression in urban scenes, and the visual difference between perspective and near-orthographic projection.

The Insight
The observed difference is caused by changes in depth Z between the camera and different parts of the face. When the camera is close, the nose is substantially nearer to the camera than the ears. Under the perspective projection model
x = f·X/Z, y = f·Y/Z
smaller values of Z produce larger projected image coordinates. This magnifies central facial features relative to those farther away, creating the familiar "wide-angle distortion."
What This Shows
When the camera is moved several meters back, the absolute depth Z of the face increases, while the relative difference in depth between the nose and ears becomes negligible. Zooming in with a longer focal length restores the framing but does not change the geometry of the scene. In this configuration, the image approaches an orthographic-like regime, and facial proportions appear more natural.

The Insight
This experiment demonstrates vanishing points and the dependence of perspective strength on camera position. When the camera is placed far from the scene and zoomed in, the viewing angles subtended by the objects become small. Parallel lines—such as fence rails or pavement edges—converge very slowly, effectively pushing their vanishing points far away. This reduces apparent depth differences and creates a "flattened" or compressed look.
What This Shows
When the camera is moved closer without zoom, the same parallel lines intersect at steeper angles and converge more strongly. Foreground and background elements change scale rapidly with depth, producing a vivid sense of three-dimensional structure. This is characteristic of true perspective projection.

Red-Line Analysis
To visualize the underlying projection model, straight edges in each scene were traced and extended. In the near, wide-angle images, the extended lines intersect at clear vanishing points, confirming perspective projection with rays meeting at a single camera center. In the far, zoomed or tightly cropped images, the same lines remain almost parallel. Because the camera subtends only a narrow visual angle, the convergence of perspective is minimized, producing an orthographic-like appearance.
What This Shows
Although these images are still produced by a perspective camera, increasing camera distance and zooming causes them to approach the orthographic limit.
