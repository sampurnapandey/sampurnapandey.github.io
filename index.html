Perspective Geometry in Real Photographs

sampurna.pandey_ug2023@ashoka.edu.in Professor Raghavendra Singh Spring 2026
This project investigates how camera position and projection geometry influence visual appearance. I photographed the same scenes from different distances while adjusting zoom to maintain similar framing. The results illustrate three classical effects from computer vision: perspective distortion in portraits, telephoto compression in urban scenes, and the visual difference between perspective and near-orthographic projection.

Exercise 1: Portrait — Perspective Distortion vs. Natural Proportions

Close-up
Zoomed from Distance
Close-up • Annotated
The observed difference is caused by changes in depth Z between my camera and different parts of the face. When I positioned the camera close, the nose was substantially nearer to the lens than the ears. Under the perspective projection model
x = f·X/Z, y = f·Y/Z
smaller values of Z produce larger projected image coordinates. This magnifies central facial features relative to those farther away, creating the familiar "wide-angle distortion."
Zoomed from Distance • Annotated
When I moved several meters back, the absolute depth Z of the face increased, while the relative difference in depth between the nose and ears became negligible. Zooming in with a longer focal length restored the framing but did not change the geometry of the scene. In this configuration, the image approaches an orthographic-like regime, and facial proportions appear more natural.

Exercise 2: Urban Scene — Telephoto Compression

Wide Angle
Telephoto
Wide Angle • Annotated
This experiment demonstrates vanishing points and the dependence of perspective strength on camera position. When I moved closer without zoom, parallel lines—such as fence rails or pavement edges—intersected at steeper angles and converged more strongly. Foreground and background elements changed scale rapidly with depth, producing a vivid sense of three-dimensional structure. This is characteristic of true perspective projection.
Telephoto • Annotated
When I placed the camera far from the scene and zoomed in, the viewing angles subtended by the objects became small. The same parallel lines converged very slowly, effectively pushing their vanishing points far away. This reduced apparent depth differences and created a "flattened" or compressed look.

Exercise 3: Perspective vs. Orthographic-Like Projection

Perspective View
Orthographic-Like View
Perspective View • Annotated
To visualize the underlying projection model, I traced and extended straight edges in each scene. In the near, wide-angle images, the extended lines intersected at clear vanishing points, confirming perspective projection with rays meeting at a single camera center.
Orthographic-Like View • Annotated
In the far, zoomed or tightly cropped images, the same lines remained almost parallel. Because the camera subtended only a narrow visual angle, the convergence of perspective was minimized, producing an orthographic-like appearance. Although these images are still produced by a perspective camera, increasing camera distance and zooming causes them to approach the orthographic limit.
